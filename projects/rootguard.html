RootGuard: Ultra-Low-Power IoT Soil Telemetry Station

Context: Personal Engineering Project · Embedded C++, ESP32-C5 RISC-V, Hardware Forensics & Autodesk Inventor

A bespoke, battery-powered soil moisture telemetry station engineered from scratch to prevent houseplant overwatering, combining 15 µA deep sleep, active RC sensor settling, schematic forensics, soldering rework, 3D CAD design, and a zero-heap-fragmentation captive web portal.

The Motivation: Saving a Monstera from “Helicopter Parenting”

  • The Problem: Houseplants, particularly the Monstera deliciosa, are notoriously vulnerable to root rot caused by overwatering. Visual and tactile top-soil checks are fundamentally unreliable because the top 2 cm of soil dries out rapidly while the deep root zone remains saturated.
  • The Objective: Rather than buying a cheap off-the-shelf plastic probe, I used the problem as a hands-on vehicle to master embedded systems from the ground up: ESP32-C5 RISC-V silicon architecture, schematic reading, hardware debugging, hand soldering, and mechanical CAD enclosure design.

System Architecture & Hardware Stack

  • Microcontroller: DFRobot FireBeetle 2 ESP32-C5 (RISC-V core).
  • Capacitive Moisture Sensing: High-frequency capacitive probe chosen over resistive pins to eliminate copper trace corrosion and DC soil electrolysis.
  • Local Display: 0.96” I2C OLED (SSD1306, 128x64) providing immediate on-device telemetry.
  • Power & Battery Telemetry: 500 mAh 3.7V LiPo cell monitored via the FireBeetle’s onboard 1M/1M high-impedance voltage divider (IO1 / GPIO 1).
  • Mechanical Housing: Custom multi-part enclosure designed in Autodesk Inventor and 3D printed with snap/screw tolerances, internal PCB bosses, battery retention, and a screen bezel.

Mechanical Engineering: Autodesk Inventor Enclosure Design

To make the station self-contained and durable enough for wet soil environments, I modeled a custom multi-part enclosure in Autodesk Inventor:

  • Tolerancing & Component Packaging: Designed dedicated internal mounting bosses for the FireBeetle PCB, a snug lower bay for the 500 mAh LiPo battery, and a flush front bezel for the 0.96” OLED screen.
  • Ergonomics & Cable Management: Integrated wire routing channels with strain relief for the capacitive probe cable, along with a tactile external button collar for mode switching.
  • First-Print Assembly: By calculating 0.25 mm press-fit clearances and accounting for 3D printer material shrinkage, the electronics, screen, and battery seated securely on the first physical print.

Hardware Forensics, Schematic Tracing & Soldering Rework

The initial prototype assembly was soldered by a lecturer, but real-world testing quickly surfaced critical electrical conflicts: floating ground references, erratic wakeups, false 100% moisture spikes, and CPU panics (Store access fault) when attempting sleep states.

Diving into the FireBeetle 2 ESP32-C5 schematics, board variant definitions (pins_arduino.h), and Espressif silicon errata revealed three major hardware bugs:

  1. Unrouted Header Pins: Multiple pins on the left-side header rail were unrouted NC (No Connection) lines, causing floating ground references during initial sensor reads.
  2. Low-Power (LP) Wakeup Domain Conflict: The external trigger button had initially been wired to D9 (GPIO 28). Schematic tracing revealed GPIO 28 is shared with the onboard BOOT switch and sits outside the ESP32-C5’s Low-Power (LP) sleep domain, preventing button wakeups.
  3. Shared LED_BUILTIN Parasitic Drain on D13 (GPIO 15): The sensor ground switch was wired to D13 (GPIO 15), which physically shares a circuit with the active-HIGH green User LED. Driving GPIO 15 OUTPUT HIGH to disconnect sensor ground during sleep inadvertently sourced 2-3 mA through the LED 24/7, draining the battery and reverse-biasing the probe.

Hands-On Rework: I learned to solder and reworked the board personally, desoldering the wake button and relocating it to a true Low-Power pin (D12 / GPIO 6). In firmware, I re-engineered the D13 (GPIO 15) ground switch to act as a true open-drain sink: driving OUTPUT LOW (0V) only when sampling, and switching to high-impedance (INPUT / Hi-Z) with pull-ups disabled during sleep, eliminating the parasitic drain down to true 0 µA.

Firmware Architecture & Ultra-Low-Power Engineering

  • True Deep Sleep (~15 µA) & Pad Isolation: Implemented ESP32-C5 Deep Sleep (esp_deep_sleep_start()) using EXT1 low-level wakeup on LP GPIO 6. Pin states on GPIO 0 (3V3_C) and GPIO 6 are latched via gpio_hold_en(). After Wire.end(), I2C lines (SDA/SCL) are explicitly floated to INPUT so the OLED is not back-powered through its ESD diodes.
  • Active RC Settling Detection (waitForSensorSettle): When powering the capacitive probe from 0V, its onboard 555 timer must charge its RC output filter from 0V (which registers as 100% wet) to steady state (~2.2V dry). Instead of an inefficient blind delay, the firmware polls the charging curve until consecutive samples plateau within <= 12 ADC counts, followed by a 16-sample interquartile trimmed-mean filter (discarding 4 lowest and 4 highest outliers, averaging the middle 8).
  • Pre-Load Battery Caching & Brownout Guard: Unloaded LiPo voltage is sampled and cached before enabling the sensor rail or Wi-Fi radio (~180 mA load) to prevent battery internal resistance voltage sag from distorting telemetry. A 10-point piecewise-linear discharge curve maps voltage to real state of charge, backed by a < 3.32V brownout guard that triggers a 12-hour deep hibernation when depleted.
  • State Persistence Across Sleep: Critical counters and calibration parameters persist in RTC_DATA_ATTR slow memory across deep-sleep cycles. If power is ever severed, an O(1) tail-seek routine (recoverStateFromFS()) restores the last sample ID and timestamp from LittleFS.

Tri-Mode Operation & Zero-Heap Web Architecture

The firmware balances power conservation with user accessibility through three operating modes:

  1. Automated Background Logging (Every 4 Hours): Headless wakeup (keeping OLED and Wi-Fi powered down), samples unloaded battery voltage, powers sensor, waits for RC settling, appends filtered reading to /history.csv on LittleFS (with automatic 128 KB log rotation to /history_old.csv), and sleeps in under 450 ms.
  2. Short Button Tap (5-Second Glance Mode): Initializes the OLED at power-saving contrast, rendering current moisture with a large 2x readout, trend badge (+3, -2, ~0), status pill (DRY!, OPTIMAL, WET), battery gauge, and a 1px animated sleep countdown bar.
  3. Long Button Hold (Wi-Fi Captive Portal): Holding the button displays a 1.8-second progress bar, then launches the Plant-Monitor SoftAP and an embedded DNS Captive Portal.

Zero-Heap Web Architecture: To prevent heap fragmentation in RAM, the web server seeks directly to the tail of /history.csv, loads the last 60 samples into a 1.6 KB stack-allocated circular buffer, and streams HTML/SVG responses via chunked HTTP transfer (PROGMEM + server.sendContent()).

Browser-Injected Time Sync: Because the board operates offline without an external hardware RTC, the web dashboard executes an automatic background JavaScript fetch (fetch('/settime?epoch=...')) upon connection. This synchronizes the ESP32-C5’s internal RTC to South African Standard Time (SAST-2) and persists the timestamp to LittleFS.

Technical Specifications Summary

  • Microcontroller: ESP32-C5 RISC-V (DFRobot FireBeetle 2)
  • Sleep Power Consumption: ~15 µA (Deep Sleep with pad latching & Hi-Z ground isolation)
  • Sensing Methodology: 555-timer high-frequency capacitive probe with active RC settling
  • ADC Filtering: 16-sample in-place interquartile trimmed-mean filter
  • Storage & Persistence: LittleFS flash filesystem with 128 KB rotation, NVS flash calibration, and RTC_DATA_ATTR memory
  • Web Architecture: ESP32 WebServer with DNS captive portal, chunked HTTP streaming, and zero heap allocations
  • CAD Software: Autodesk Inventor (Parametric multi-part assembly)
